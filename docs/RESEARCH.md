# Research Notes

## Sources reviewed

- Stripe: direct positioning, concrete proof, customer stories, and product-led explanations.
- Raycast: short memorable promise, emotional framing, and product-specific visual demonstrations.
- Arc: calm visual language and an immediate explanation of why the product exists.
- Crisp and Tidio: objection handling, operational walkthroughs, and evidence attached to claims.
- Refactoring UI: hierarchy, spacing, restrained borders, readable type, and designing with tactics rather than decoration.

## Findings applied to Whano

1. The first screen must answer what Whano does without requiring the visitor to understand AI, automation, or WhatsApp infrastructure.
2. A product-specific thread is stronger than an abstract dashboard screenshot for this product. The customer experience is the product.
3. Trust comes from transparent behavior and specific explanations, not invented logos, testimonials, or performance numbers.
4. The page needs to explain the post-checkout workflow in order: order received, confirmation, change, shipping, review.
5. A sparse editorial grid creates distinction without adding visual noise. Lines and spacing carry structure; cards are used only where the content needs containment.
6. Arabic must be written for Egyptian customers, not translated word-for-word from English. Colloquial product copy and formal legal copy serve different jobs.
